# Eidolon Product Brief

## Positioning

**Eidolon — the embodiment layer for your agent.**

Eidolon gives an existing agent a persistent, native presence while real work happens.

The terminal remains the interface to the work.
Eidolon becomes the interface to the worker.

## First audience

Coding-agent, local-model, and persona-programming power users.

The same core experience should remain accessible to ordinary users through simpler packaging and defaults, without weakening the underlying system.

## Product promise

One persistent persona visibly and truthfully inhabits the agents, models, sessions, and tools acting on the user’s behalf.

She talks, moves, breathes, reacts, and remains present during real work—not inside a separate novelty chat box.

## Initial product boundary

Eidolon observes and embodies existing agent sessions.

It does not initially replace:

- the terminal;
- the agent runtime;
- dense work interfaces;
- provider inference;
- general tool orchestration.

## Development sequence

1. Build the native agent-embodiment product.
2. Architect toward a complete persistent-persona platform.
3. Earn the right to become a protocol and ecosystem.

## Long-term direction

Eidolon may eventually become the durable runtime where identity, memory, providers, tools, agents, bodies, and applications agree on interfaces.

The persona must remain portable across:

- models;
- providers;
- sessions;
- tools;
- bodies;
- applications.

## Competitive advantage

Not merely native rendering.

Not merely an animated avatar connected to an LLM.

Eidolon combines:

- truthful agent-state embodiment;
- low-latency presence;
- persistent persona continuity;
- provider independence;
- native desktop integration;
- direct connection to real work.

## Non-goals for the first release

- replacing the terminal;
- implementing a complete agent platform;
- training a first-party general model;
- building a provider marketplace;
- reproducing every AIRI subsystem;
- creating a universal protocol before real product use proves its requirements.
